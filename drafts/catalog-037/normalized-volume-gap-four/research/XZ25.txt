                                                                                  1. Introduction
                                              The algebraic K-stability of Fano varieties has been a very active research topic in
                                           recent years. The local analogue is a stability theory for Kawamata log terminal (klt)
                                           singularities. There were two roots from earlier analytic works: the first is the work
                                           [MSY08], which shows that for a klt singularity x ∈ X with a torus T action, the existence
                                           of the Sasaki-Einstein metric along a Reeb vector field ξ implies that ξ minimizes a
                                           normalized volume function defined among all Reeb vector fields; the second one is the
                                           study of the Ricci-flat metric tangent cone for a singularity on the Kähler-Einstein Fano
                                           variety which is the Gromov-Hausdorff limit of Kähler-Einstein manifolds [DS17].
                                              The general algebraic local stability theory was initiated in [Li18]. It centers around
                                           the problem of minimizing the normalized volume function

                                          2. Preliminaries
   Throughout this paper, we work over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic
0. All algebraic varieties are assumed to be quasi-projective. We follow the standard
terminology from [KM98, Kol13].
   A singularity x ∈ (X, ∆) consists of a normal affine variety X, an effective Q-divisor ∆
(could be 0) on X and a closed point x ∈ X. A divisor over a singularity x ∈ (X, ∆) is a
prime divisor E ⊆ Y on some proper birational model µ : Y → X such that µ(E) = {x}.
   For an effective Q-divisor ∆ on a normal variety, an effective Q-divisor ∆0 is called a
Q-subdivisor of ∆ if ∆0 ≤ ∆.
   Let (X, ∆) → U be a projective morphism from a klt pair (X, ∆) such that −KX − ∆
is ample. A (global ) Q-complement is an effective Q-divisor Γ, such that (X, ∆ + Γ) is
log canonical and KX + ∆ + Γ ∼Q 0. A (local ) Q-complement of a singularity x ∈ (X, ∆)
is an effective Q-divisor Γ such that (X, ∆ + Γ) is lc and x is an lc center.1
   Given a ring R, an (N-)graded sequence a• = {ak }k∈N of ideals is a set of ideals ak ⊆ R
(k ∈ N) satisfying that ak · ak′ ⊆ ak+k′ .
2.1. Qdlt pairs. The concept of qdlt pairs is introduced in [dFKX17, Definition 35].
Definition 2.1. Let D ⊆ X be a reduced divisor on a normal variety. We say (X, D) is
toroidal if for any x ∈ X there exists an SNC pair x′ ∈ (X ′ , D ′ ) and a finite abelian group
G acting on it preserving every irreducible component of D ′ , such that the G-action is
free in codimension one and Zariski locally we have
                            (x ∈ (X, D)) ∼= (x′ ∈ (X ′ , D ′ )) /G.
Definition 2.2 ([dFKX17, Definition 35]). A qdlt pair (X, ∆) is a log pair such that
there exists an open set U ⊂ X such that ∆|U is reduced, (U, ∆|U ) is toroidal, and the log
discrepancy AX,∆ (F ) > 0 for any prime divisor F over X whose center on X is contained
in X \U. Note that when ⌊∆⌋ is irreducible, being qdlt is equivalent to being plt. Similarly
we can define sub-qdlt pairs by allowing negative coefficients in ∆.
  Qdlt pairs behave very similar to dlt pairs. We list a few properties of this kind.

Lemma 2.4. Let X be a normal variety, and D a Weil divisor on X. Let H be an ample
divisor. Then the base locus of |mH + D| for m ≫ 0 is the non-Cartier locus of D.
Proof. This should be well-known to experts. First we show that every closed point
x ∈ X in the non-Cartier locus of D is also in the base locus of |mH + D|. In fact,
if there exists G ∈ |mH + D| such that x 6∈ Supp(G) then G is clearly Cartier at x,
hence so is D ∼ G − mH, a contradiction. Next we take some m ≫ 0 such that the
sheaf OX (D + mH) is globally generated. For every x ∈ X where D is Cartier, we have
OX (D + mH) ⊗ k(x) ∼ = k(x), while global generation induces a surjection
                      H 0 (X, OX (D + mH)) ։ OX (D + mH) ⊗ k(x).
Thus we may find a section s ∈ H 0 (X, OX (D + mH)) that does not vanish at x; in other
words, we get a divisor G ∈ |mH + D| that does not pass through x. As x is arbitrary,
this implies that the base locus of |mH + D| is contained in the non-Cartier locus of D,
and we are done.                                                                      

                      3. Lc places of special Q-complements
   The concept of (Q-)complements was first introduced in [Sho92]. However, it is known
that unlike the divisorial case, an arbitrary lc place v of a Q-complement with rational rank
larger than one does not necessarily have a finitely generated associated graded algebra
(see e.g. [AZ22, Theorem 4.16] and [LXZ22, Theorem 6.1]). We need to look at a smaller
class of Q-complements, which we call special Q-complements. The goal of this section
is then two-folds. First we relate minimizers of the normalized volume function to lc
places of special Q-complement. More precisely, we will prove that for any klt singularity
x ∈ (X, ∆), any minimizer of the normalized volume function vol    c is a monomial lc place
of a special Q-complement Γ with respect to some log smooth model (Y, E) (see Lemma
3.2). Next we will introduce the concept of Kollár models as a higher rank analog of
Kollár components. As a crucial step towards the finite generation statement, we show
that any monomial lc place of a special Q-complement with respect to some log smooth
model can also be realized on a Kollár model (Theorem 3.14). In the next section, we will
prove finite generation by analyzing degenerations of Kollár models.
