Theorem 1.1 — Generating the entire Iitaka field in one degree

Fix \(d\ge5\) and a finite set \(\Phi\subset[0,1]\cap\mathbb Q\). Let \(X\) be a normal integral projective \(d\)-fold over an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic zero, and let \(B\) be an effective rational boundary. Assume that \((X,B)\) is lc, the nonzero coefficients of \(B\) belong to \(\Phi\), \(D=K_X+B\) is \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier, and \(\kappa(X,D)\ge0\). The authors assert that there is a positive integer \(m\), depending only on \(d,\Phi\), such that

\[ V_m(D):=H^0(X,\mathcal O_X(\lfloor mD\rfloor))\ne0, \qquad F_m(D)=K(D)\subset k(X) \]

(Theorem 1.1, p. 2). Here \(F_m(D)\) is generated by all ratios of degree-\(m\) sections, and \(K(D)\) by section ratios in all positive degrees. The conclusion identifies subfields of \(k(X)\), rather than merely their transcendence degrees. It is an existence statement for \(m\), without a numerical bound or a uniform Cartier index for \(K_X+B\) itself. The coefficient set in the main theorem is finite.

Theorem 4.2 — Valuative weights over a curve

Let \(C\) be a smooth complex projective curve, \(K=\mathbb C(C)\), and \(z\) a rational uniformizer at \(c\in C\). Suppose that \(V/K\) is normal, geometrically integral, projective of dimension \(s\), that \((V,B)\) is geometrically lc with an effective rational boundary, and that a rational \(m\)-canonical form \(\phi\) satisfies \(\operatorname{div}_V\phi+mB=0\). Taking all divisorial valuations \(E\) over \(c\), set

\[ w_z(\phi)=\inf_E\frac{\operatorname{ord}_E(\phi\wedge(dz/z)^{\otimes m})+m}{m\operatorname{ord}_E z}. \]

Theorem 4.2, pp. 7–8 asserts the existence of \(q(s,m)>0\) with \(q(s,m)w_z(\phi)\in\mathbb Z\). This integer is independent of the curve, pair and form; no separate coefficient set is fixed. The assumptions already make \(mB\) integral.

Proposition 4.5, pp. 9–10 first produces a relative dlt model \((N,T+H)\), where \(T\) is the reduced special fibre and \(H\) the horizontal boundary, on which the weight appears in an actual divisor identity. Removing negative crepant coefficients introduces an effective exceptional error. After a relative MMP this error is vertical; comparison with a relative principal divisor makes it a scalar multiple of the fibre, and the threshold defining the weight forces the scalar to vanish. The relative MMP is justified through Lemma 3.1, pp. 6–7: add a sufficiently positive divisor from the base, use projective log abundance, and use the length bound for lc extremal rays to force every contraction to be vertical.

If \(H\) has a coefficient-one component \(S\), dlt adjunction and residue reduce the dimension. Applying SD on the generic fibre bounds the finite degree in the Stein factorization \(S\to C_S\to C\) by \(D_s\). With ramification index \(e\le D_s\), the residue weight is \(e\,w_z(\phi)\), so \(q(s-1,m)\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,D_s)\) clears the original denominator (Lemma 4.6, pp. 10–11). When no coefficient-one horizontal component remains, the prepared generic fibre is \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein klt. That case requires a separate character bound.

Proposition 6.7 — Eliminating covering degrees through characters

The decomposition in MW gives, after a finite quasi-étale cover, a rationally connected block carrying the boundary, an abelian variety, Calabi–Yau blocks and irreducible symplectic blocks. The other blocks carry no boundary. Vanishing of logarithmic tangent vector fields on the rationally connected block (Lemma 5.2, pp. 12–13) supports the comparison of product decompositions. The argument does not assume that a finite Galois closure is itself a product. Proposition 5.4, pp. 14–15 recovers block function fields from central idempotents in the endomorphism algebra of the tangent sheaf, kills permutations by an \(s!\)-th power, and constructs a common finite comparison cover with actions on its blocks.

After semistable base change and normalization to weight zero, write \(\phi=a\bigwedge_i\eta_i^{\otimes m/p_i}\), with \(p_i=m\) on the rationally connected block and \(p_i=1\) otherwise. If \(\ell\) is the ramification index and \(b=s!\), equations (6.2)–(6.3), p. 19 give

\[ \ell w_z(\phi)=\frac{\operatorname{ord}_{c'}a}{m}, \qquad \zeta^{b\operatorname{ord}_{c'}a}\prod_i\lambda_i^{m/p_i}=1 \]

For abelian blocks, finite-rank integral cohomology bounds the character order. On the other blocks, Du Bois base change compares \(H^j(\mathcal O)\) on the generic and special fibres, and coherent Lefschetz traces produce a fixed point for a bounded power. A further bounded power fixes a coefficient-one component through that point. Adjunction and residue on this normal component, followed by the normal lc index theorem U on its cyclic quotient, kill the residue character uniformly (Lemmas 6.5–6.6, pp. 21–22). An slc index theorem for the whole special fibre is not the input here.

A common \(Q(s,m)\) with \(\lambda_i^Q=1\) for every block yields \(\ell\mid bQ\operatorname{ord}_{c'}a\). Consequently

\[ q_{\rm klt}(s,m)=m\,s!\,Q(s,m), \qquad q_{\rm klt}(s,m)w_z(\phi)\in\mathbb Z. \]

This is Proposition 6.7, p. 23. The decisive connection is the cancellation of the uncontrolled ramification degree introduced by semistable reduction.

Theorem 7.2 — Moduli denominators with an exact presentation fixed

Fix \(d\ge1\) and a finite rational coefficient set \(\Phi\). Let \(f:X\to Z\) be a contraction of normal complex projective varieties, with \(\dim X\le d\) and \(\dim Z>0\). Assume that \((X,B)\) is lc, \(B\ge0\) has nonzero coefficients in \(\Phi\), and the \(\mathbb Q\)-Cartier divisor \(K_X+B\) is \(\mathbb Q\)-linearly equivalent to \(f^*L\). Proposition 7.1, pp. 23–24 supplies a uniform \(p_0(d,\Phi)\) and a rational function \(\psi\) such that

\[ K_X+B+\frac1{p_0}\operatorname{div}\psi=f^*D_Z, \qquad D_Z\sim_{\mathbb Q}L, \qquad D_Z=K_Z+B_Z+M_Z \]

For the moduli b-divisor defined by an exact presentation of this degree, Theorem 7.2, p. 25 asserts that a uniform \(p(d,\Phi)\), divisible by \(p_0\), makes \(pM_W\) nef Cartier on every smooth projective determination of the moduli part. This does not bound denominators of representatives modified by arbitrary rational principal divisors, nor assert b-semiampleness.

The normal lc index theorem U on the geometric generic fibre, followed by Hilbert 90 in the same degree, supplies the uniform \(p_0\). Qualitative moduli descent uses FG, while the DCC property of discriminant coefficients uses HMX. A transverse curve through the generic point of a prime divisor \(P\subset W\) preserves its coefficient \(\alpha\) and threshold \(t_P\), including negative coefficients in the crepant subboundary. The relative form has weight \(w_z(\phi)=\alpha-1+t_P\), differing from the moduli coefficient only by the integral coefficient of \(K_W\) (Lemma 7.3, pp. 25–27). A common multiple of \(q(s,p_0)\) over all relative dimensions makes \(pM_W\) integral and hence Cartier on smooth \(W\).

When \(L\) is big, effective birationality BZ applies on the base. Negative exceptional crepant coefficients are first replaced by the reduced exceptional boundary to produce an effective generalized pair. For \(p_0\mid n\), the complete section spaces satisfy

\[ H^0(X,\lfloor n(K_X+B)\rfloor) =\psi^{n/p_0}f^*H^0(Z,\lfloor nD_Z\rfloor) \]

Lemma 8.1, pp. 27–28 proves this identification and its birational invariance for rounded divisors, without introducing the Cartier index of the total space. A ratio in any degree can be moved to a degree divisible by \(p_0\) by multiplying numerator and denominator by a power of one section. Thus the entire Iitaka field equals the base field (Proposition 8.2, pp. 28–29). Finally, LA and TX provide a good model; \(\kappa>0\) reduces to the big-base case, while \(\kappa=0\) uses U. Lemma 8.3 and the final proof, pp. 29–30 handle extension to arbitrary algebraically closed characteristic-zero fields through base change of sections and fields.
