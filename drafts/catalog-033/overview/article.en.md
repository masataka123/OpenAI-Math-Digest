[Projective Hodge lines, Theorem 1.1 · p. 2][PH] and [Orbifold Iitaka, Corollary 6.3 · p. 41][OI] prove the same ordinary characteristic-zero subadditivity statement by different routes. The latter also includes the logarithmic statement. The ordinary claim is a zero-boundary case of that logarithmic conclusion; this inclusion of results does not create a proof dependency. Compare [the two-base interpolation](https://masataka123.github.io/OpenAI-Math-Digest/en/papers/projective-hodge-lines/#proof-3) with [the characteristic-zero transfer](https://masataka123.github.io/OpenAI-Math-Digest/en/papers/orbifold-logarithmic-iitaka/#proof-4).

[Projective Hodge lines, Lemma 8.4 / Theorem 8.1 · pp. 28–29][PH] is an alternative adjoint comparison in [Whole-fiber variation, Remark 3.17 · p. 25][WV]. Tensor replacement must be performed again on the restriction; the receiving proof uses the direct comparison. Separately, additivity and the variation lower bound recover the nonnegative-base branch for smooth families with finite nonnegative fiber Kodaira dimension ([comparison · p. 5][WV]); they do not cover negative bases or every special base.

Weak positivity and interpolation in Orbifold Iitaka and Projective Hodge lines are similar methods with different Hodge-line hypotheses. The root-line calculations in Orbifold Iitaka and Kähler B-semiampleness also use different boundaries: orbifold inf-multiplicity versus the lct discriminant. [Kähler B-semiampleness · p. 3][BS] explicitly avoids the orbifold Iitaka theorem; this is not a claim that all possible inter-paper dependencies have been investigated. Its [whole proof overview](https://masataka123.github.io/OpenAI-Math-Digest/en/papers/kahler-b-semiampleness/#proof-1) follows its own stabilization and product-factor route.

The connection-form Ax–Schanuel argument in Reverse log additivity is recorded as a method background: the manuscript gives the semisimple case it needs. The [external-input discussion](https://masataka123.github.io/OpenAI-Math-Digest/en/papers/reverse-logarithmic-additivity/#dependencies) distinguishes this from importing an external theorem.
