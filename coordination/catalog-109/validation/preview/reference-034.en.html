The connection between the strands

Log Abundance supplies qualitative good models to Schnell and to the effective-degree papers. The fourfold route combines nonvanishing, lifting and relative denominators. In the Kähler strand, projective good models become an anchor for analytic induction; its subadditivity and MMP premises remain visible.

1. Nonvanishing and abundance: from a first section to generation

Log Abundance supplies all-dimensional good models. Alongside it, the fourfold papers separate nonvanishing from lifting. Schnell is an application of the geometry of the resulting good model.

1. Metrics extend boundary sections; nonzero sections need another input

Fourfold nonvanishing uses Minimal metrics, Theorems 1.1–1.2, for zero-Lelong metrics and interior injectivity on the endpoint residual bundles. These supply surjectivity of restriction. Three-dimensional semi-dlt abundance and descent separately produce a nonzero section on the whole boundary; combining the two steps gives nonvanishing upstairs.

2. The remaining nonvanishing argument imports Log Abundance tools

The moving-jet argument imports Log Abundance, Lemmas 7.1–7.5, to control centres, degrees and covers. Theorem 9.1 then rules out coexistence of the constructed jets, flag and movable curve by Frobenius comparison. These are auxiliary inputs, rather than an application of Log Abundance’s abundance main theorem.

3. Combine nonvanishing with abundance after nonvanishing

Supported lifting, Theorem 1.2, proves fourfold semiampleness assuming nonnegative Kodaira dimension. Fourfold nonvanishing, Corollary 1.2, supplies the first section, giving Supported lifting, Corollary 1.4. A separate input to its conditional Theorem 1.3 is FN Lemma 8.1: rational linear algebra converting an effective real presentation to a rational one. The two uses must be distinguished.

Log Abundance, Corollary 11.2, supplies a model with semiample canonical divisor and an effective exceptional comparison. Schnell transfers sections to bound the Kodaira dimension, then uses the mixed intersections of Lemma 2.3 for the remaining inequality. Model existence is the imported input; the subsequent intersection argument is carried out in Schnell.

2. Effectivity: from existence of sections to a uniform degree

Good models alone do not bound the degree uniformly. The arguments separately control generic-fibre indices, moduli denominators on the base and degrees of finite covers. The diagram shows the six papers addressing these tasks and an input from Log Abundance.

1. Separate qualitative good models from uniform denominators

In Fourfold Iitaka, Log Abundance supplies nonvanishing from pseudo-effectivity and Supported lifting makes the nef adjoint semiample after nonvanishing. Relative denominators, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 6.1, control relative denominators and complete systems on a big base uniformly. The qualitative step supplies the fibration; the effective step captures its function field in a uniform degree.

2. Induct on normal lc indices and Iitaka degrees together

Uniform Iitaka combines Log Abundance good models with Stein degree, Theorem 1.1, bounding constant-field extensions of boundary components and hence norm descent. Arbitrary-dimensional chain lemmas from Fourfold Iitaka lead from covering families to estimates on leaves. The output is a uniform complete system generating the full Iitaka field, not just an image of the right dimension.

3. Use normal indices on the generic fibre to obtain log Iitaka effectivity

Log Iitaka applies Uniform Iitaka, Theorem 1.2, to the generic fibre, fixing a uniform trivializing multiple and an exact pullback presentation. Log Abundance supplies the semiample model, while Stein degree controls finite extensions in the residue induction. The finite rational coefficient set of the log Iitaka main theorem should be distinguished from the rational DCC coefficient scope of the normal lc index theorem.

4. For slc pairs, glue normalization trivializations across the conductor

slc indices applies the normal lc index theorem on normalization components, but descent to the original slc pair needs more. Product covers and stability under further covers from Log Iitaka, the rational-image lemma from Uniform Iitaka, and Log Abundance good models enter the character bounds. Residues and cycles must then be made compatible. The slc paper itself supplies the final conductor gluing.

3. Kähler: retain dimension, singularities and premises

Conditional all-dimensional lc abundance, conditional klt fourfold models, and klt fourfold abundance after nonvanishing have different scopes. Dimension-free auxiliary lemmas are drawn separately from the main theorems of the papers containing them.

1. In dimension four, separate MMP, nonvanishing and subsequent abundance

Conditional Kähler models proves nonvanishing at the specified nef MMP endpoint, then invokes Kähler after nonvanishing, Theorem 1.1, for semiampleness. This is Assumption 2.4. Orbifold subadditivity (Assumption 2.2) and the specified MMP (Assumption 2.3) remain necessary; the after-nonvanishing theorem alone does not supply all premises.

Connection details:Kähler after nonvanishing → Conditional Kähler models · Theorem 1.1

2. For all-dimensional lc abundance, connect a projective anchor to analytic induction

Kähler abundance, Proposition 2.10, uses the full Log Abundance induction for projective good models and effective exceptional comparison. The argument then splits into a positive-dimensional fibration case and a simple case of algebraic dimension zero. Theorem 6.1 in the latter gives meromorphic nonvanishing, not directly a holomorphic section. Signed-boundary rigidity and lower-dimensional induction return to generation of the original nef adjoint. Assumption 1.1 remains in the main theorem.

Connection details:Log Abundance → Kähler abundance · Proposition 2.5 / Theorem 9.6 / §10

3. The inputs extending beyond dimension four are specified lemmas

Conditional Kähler models, Lemmas 7.16–7.17, give dimension-free strata adjunction and strict comparison, used in Kähler abundance, Theorem 3.13. Torsion-freeness in Kähler after nonvanishing, Lemma 7.1, is another input: Kähler abundance, Lemma 4.3, uses it to pass from residue matching to section extension. Neither connection applies a fourfold main theorem in arbitrary dimension.
