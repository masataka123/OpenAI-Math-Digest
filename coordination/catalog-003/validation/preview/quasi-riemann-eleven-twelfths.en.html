The Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis

Sextic mean squares, cubic theta transformation, and finite iteration after cube inversion

The manuscript claims a zero-free half-plane for finite-order Hecke L-functions over $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$, with a boundary independent of conductor and height. It embeds a Möbius sum in a sextically twisted family and passes through cubic theta coefficients to a quadratic large sieve. This article describes the argument and its direct inputs; it does not certify an independent verification of the complete proof.

1. Main results

Theorem 1.1 — A zero-free half-plane of fixed width

For every finite-order Hecke character $\nu$ over $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})$, $L_K(s,\nu)$ has no zeros in $$\operatorname{Re}s>\frac{11}{12}.$$ Consequently every Dirichlet L-function $L(s,\chi)$, including $\zeta(s)$, has no zeros in the same half-plane. There is no restriction on the imaginary part; the boundary line is excluded. For a principal character, $s=1$ is a pole: nonvanishing here does not assert holomorphy.

Corollary 1.2 — A uniform prime-number estimate in progressions

Let $\pi(x;q,a)=\#\{p\le x:p\equiv a\pmod q\}$ and let $\varphi$ denote Euler's totient. For every $x\ge2$, the manuscript claims $$\sup_{\substack{1\le q\le x\\(a,q)=1}}\left|\pi(x;q,a)-\frac1{\varphi(q)}\int_2^x\frac{dt}{\log t}\right|\ll x^{11/12}\log x,$$ with an absolute, effective implied constant.

2. Proof of Theorem 1.1 — From a mean square to Mellin nonvanishing

Proof target：Theorem 1.1 · p. 2

Proof route. Poisson summation converts Möbius coefficients into cubic Gauss sums. A theta transformation controls a cube-completed sum; finite iteration removes the extra cubes. Prime sixth-power rows then extract the exponent $11/12$, and a Mellin transform excludes zeros. Write $N$ for the absolute norm, $S$ for a fixed set containing the primes above $2,3$ and the conductor of $\nu$, $\chi_n(u)=(u/n)_6$, and $W$ for a smooth weight of fixed compact support.

1. The theta transformation produces a quadratic large-sieve family

Set $A_u(D)=\sum_{(n,S)=1}\mu(n)\nu(n)\chi_n(u)W(Nn/D)$. Poisson summation in $u$, followed by the Gauss–Jacobi identities in Lemma 4.1, converts $\mu(n)\gamma_{-1}(n)$ into $a_\xi(n)=\overline{\alpha(n)}\gamma_2(n)\xi(n)$, with $\alpha(n)=n/|n|$ and $\xi$ a character of a fixed ray class group. Extracting common divisors introduces an auxiliary variable $f$. The exact family is $$E(\mathcal H,X,F)=\frac1{XF}\sum_{Nf\asymp F}^{*}\sum_{0<Nk\le\mathcal H}\left|\sum_n^{*}a_\xi(n)\chi_n(k)\chi_n(f)^4W(Nn/X)\right|^2.$$ Stars restrict to squarefree primary elements coprime to $S$. Proposition 4.5 transfers a bound for this family back to the original mean square.

Lemma 4.1; Proposition 4.5 · pp. 13–18

Cubic theta coefficients have squarefree-times-cube support $nb^3$. Adding the cube indices produces $T(X;k,f)$, to which theta automorphy applies. At each prime in the squarefree row, the transformed exponent is $-1-2\equiv3\pmod6$, hence quadratic. Lemma 6.3 fixes cusp coefficient sequences within each ray class before GL13 is applied. Lemma 6.5 checks squarefree rows and columns, finite reciprocity corrections, and primitive conductors. Separating repeated prime factors then gives $$\sum_{0<Nk\ll\mathcal H}|T(X;k,f)|^2\ll D^\varepsilon\|W\|_{C^J}^2\left(\mathcal H+\frac{\mathcal H^2Nf}{X}\right).$$ DR24 supplies theta coefficients and the transformation framework; its GRH-conditional prime asymptotic is not used. The general sextic-twist transformation and its uniformity belong to the present manuscript's Proposition 6.2 and Lemma 6.3.

2. Finite iteration closes the cube-inversion remainder

Cancellation prevents simply retaining the $b=1$ term of the completed sum. Complete multiplicativity allows Möbius inversion. The completed estimate handles inverse divisors up to $H_c^3=\min\{X,X^2/\mathcal H^2\}$. Larger cube factors leave a shorter column length $L_b=X/(Nb)^3$. Proposition 5.4 enlarges a nonnegative row range between two Poisson transformations. With target $\Sigma=XF$, the resulting family satisfies $$\mathcal H'\le\frac{\mathcal H L_b}{\Sigma F},\qquad \frac{\mathcal H'}{\Sigma'}\le\frac{\mathcal H}{\Sigma},\qquad \Sigma'=X'F'\le L_b.$$ If $\mathcal H\le\Sigma D^{-\kappa}$, then $Nb>H_c$ implies $\mathcal H'<D^{-2\kappa}\mathcal H$. Section 5.5 inducts on $\mathcal H\le D^{j\kappa}$ to obtain $E\ll D^\varepsilon\Sigma\|W\|_{C^J}^2$. Support intervals enlarge and derivative orders increase, but the number of iterations depends only on $\kappa,C_0$. Section 5.6 takes $\kappa=\vartheta/2$ to recover the original mean square.

3. Sixth-power rows give $11/12$; a zero gives a contradiction

For fixed $0<\vartheta\le1/10$, Proposition 3.1 gives $\sum_{0<Nu\le D^{1+\vartheta}}|A_u(D)|^2\ll D^{2+\vartheta+\varepsilon}$. Put $Y=D^{(1+\vartheta)/6}$. For primary prime generators with $Y/2<Np\le Y$, the identity $\chi_n(p^6)=1_{p\nmid n}$ gives $A_{p^6}(D)=A_1(D)+O_W(D/Y)$. The prime ideal theorem in the fixed field supplies about $Y/\log Y$ rows, yielding $$|A_1(D)|^2\ll D^{11/6+5\vartheta/6+\varepsilon}+D^{5/3-\vartheta/3}.$$ Choosing $\vartheta$ for each requested exponent loss gives $A_1(D)\ll_{\nu,S,W,\varepsilon}D^{11/12+\varepsilon}$. The constant may depend on $\nu$. The same exponent for each character, rather than a conductor-uniform mean-square constant, gives the final conductor-independent boundary.

Proposition 3.1; (3.2)–(3.3) · pp. 11–12

If $\operatorname{Re}\rho>11/12$ were a zero, choose $W(y)=y^{-\rho}\phi(y)$ with $0\ne\phi\ge0$ and $\phi\in C_c^\infty((1,2))$. The Mellin integral $M_W(s)=\int_0^\infty A_1(D)D^{-s}\,dD/D$ is holomorphic in this half-plane. In $\operatorname{Re}s>1$ it satisfies $L_K^S(s,\nu)M_W(s)=\widehat W(s)$; meromorphic continuation and the identity theorem extend this relation. Evaluation at $\rho$ contradicts $0=\widehat W(\rho)=\int\phi(y)\,dy/y>0$. Removed Euler factors are nonzero for $\operatorname{Re}s>0$.

4. Descent to Dirichlet functions and the point $s=1$

Quadratic base change gives, up to finitely many Euler factors nonzero in $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, $$L_K(s,\chi\circ N)=L(s,\chi)L(s,\chi\chi_{-3}).$$ Away from $s=1$ no pole can cancel a zero of either factor. At $s=1$, if one factor is principal, the other primitive function is $L(s,\chi_{-3})$; Dirichlet nonvanishing $L(1,\chi_{-3})>0$ excludes pole–zero cancellation. Extra Euler factors for imprimitive characters also do not vanish in this region.
